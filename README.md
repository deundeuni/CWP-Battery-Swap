## 0. Designer's Philosophical Declaration (Designer's Independent Conception & Prior Art Respect)

Waiting for electric vehicles to charge felt like lost time. Could we save time by adopting a swappable battery system? However, plugging in a heavy battery pack with high impact could damage the vehicle. What if we engaged it slowly, like a seesaw or bicycle gears? This was the starting point of the design.

The direction of this technical combination was conceived entirely by the natural person designer (deundeuni), defining a universal heavy payload docking architecture based on known differential reduction and rotary docking mechanisms. This system respects the engineering achievements of prior researchers and patent holders, explicitly disclosing the application of known principles as specific embodiment parameter combinations.

### 0.1 Background & Public Domain Combination

This method is not a newly created foundational technology, but a combinatory application of public domain technologies known for over a century.

* **Seesaw / Lever Principle** (Standard Mechanics)
* **Centrifugal Force / Rotational Stability** (Standard Physics)
* **Bicycle Gear Ratio** (Standard Machine Elements: 60T/61T -> 0.016rpm example)(Principle: N/(N+1) differential, N is any natural number)
* **Space Docking System** (Standard Docking Mechanisms)
* **V-groove/U-groove & Dovetail/Pin-Socket Alignment** (Standard Machine Elements: Lathe centers, mold guides, drawer rails)

### 0.2 Embodiment Combination Example (Illustrative, Non-limiting)

This is a simple illustrative example to facilitate understanding of how the combination operates. Changes in sequence or numerical figures fall within the scope of this prior art. ('EV/Battery' in this example can be substituted with any heavy payload or vehicle over 500kg, such as modular housing, disaster shelters, agricultural machinery modules, and logistics pallets)

1. **Approach:** An EV (or heavy module carrier) aligns and approaches the swapping station similar to space docking.
2. **Load Distribution:** Distributing and supporting battery (or heavy module) weight via seesaw/lever principles.
3. **Low-Speed Engagement:** Reducing relative engagement speed to low levels (e.g., ~0.016rpm) via differential gear ratios (e.g., 60T/61T) for cushioned docking (Applicable to EV battery packs, modular housing units, disaster shelters, agricultural payloads, and logistics pallets over 500kg).
4. **Alignment Lock:** Securing position via groove structures to achieve docking precision.

### 0.3 Groove Alignment & Swap-Rack Structure

* **Both-Side Groove Method:** Engaging grooves on both sides of the battery/heavy module with corresponding body grooves for 2-axis constraint and high-precision alignment.
* **One-Side Groove Method:** Constraining one side while leaving clearance on the opposite side for 1-axis constraint and assembly error absorption, enabling rapid swapping.
* **Swap-Rack Detachable Mechanism:** Single-side extraction (One-side out) -> Transfer -> Dual-side insertion (Both-side in) sequence for attachment/detachment; charging, storage, and inspection are performed separately inside the station.
* **Low-Impact Pressing Mechanism:** Applying gradual pressure starting from a predefined offset distance (e.g., ~100mm prior) to minimize physical impact.
* **Non-Limitation Declaration (Core):** All groove shapes (V, U, C, T, dovetail, pin-socket male/female guides), gear ratios (60T/61T), speeds (0.016rpm), distances (100mm), drive mechanisms (motor/pneumatic/hydraulic/manual/lever), and slot counts (8 slots) described herein are non-limiting examples. Variant shapes, numerical modifications, and alternative drive sources remain within the scope of this prior art.

### 0.4 Rotary Swapping Stage Integration Example

This docking mechanism can be combined with a rotary station.

* **Configuration:** Central rotary hub bearing assembly, rotary platform, dual-side lever mechanism (pivot/linear actuator), dual-side docking grooves (self-aligning chamfer grooves).
* **Operation:** Rotation -> Alignment -> Cushioned Docking -> Lock sequence for automated swapping.
* **Non-Limitation:** Slot counts, platform geometry, rotation directions (CW/CCW), and lever structures may vary without departing from this technological scope.

## 3.5 System Integration - CWP 3-Hardware & Survival Architecture

This differential reduction docking mechanism operates organically in combination with the three core CWP hardware mechanisms and upper survival architectures to form a zero-downtime survival-oriented swapping station.

* **Mechanical Self-Alignment (`CWP-Rolling-Self-Align-Battery-Swap-System`):** Interfacing with V-groove and caster manual/self-alignment mechanisms (Types A/B/C/S) to physically absorb entry tolerance errors (e.g., ±5mm or more) and guide the pack into the precise docking zone.
* **Differential Speed Low-Impact Docking (`CWP-Battery-Swap` - This Technology):** Utilizing N/(N+1) differential gear ratios (e.g., 60T/61T) and a rotary stage to slow down relative engagement speed to extremely low levels (e.g., ~0.016rpm) aiming for cushioned docking.
* **Electromagnetic Clamping & Secure Latching (`CWP-Clamping-Battery-Swap-System`):** Interfacing with universal EPM magnetic clamping modules, dual locking pins, and 3-layer cushion structures to achieve unpowered permanent magnetic holding and emergency release capability.
* **Physical Emergency Detachment (`0.1ms HW Intercept` / `LAST-LIGHT` Integration):** Upon emergency events such as power outages or fire, a 0.1ms suppression signal disengages differential clutches and EPM clamps, enabling manual and unpowered emergency detachment.
* **Computational Control Survival (`chiplet-apu-multi-system-survival-architecture`):** Interfacing with distributed control (CCS) and multi-chiplet control architecture to ensure swapping control logic continues operating even if a control chiplet fails.
